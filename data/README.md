# Data

Tidy inputs for the analysis. Every table is a UTF-8 CSV with LF line endings,
one row per observation and snake_case column names; percentages run from 0 to
100. GitHub shows the CSV files as searchable tables and
[`geo/provinces.geojson`](geo/provinces.geojson) as a map.

## Files

| File | Rows | Contents |
|---|---:|---|
| [`provinces.csv`](provinces.csv) | 17 | The 17 metropolitan-level local governments, keyed by their official code |
| [`parties.csv`](parties.csv) | 17 | Parties, independents and "other", with the grouping and colour used in figures |
| [`elections.csv`](elections.csv) | 3 | The three elections |
| [`offices.csv`](offices.csv) | 3 | The kinds of local office in `elected_officials_share.csv` |
| [`vote_share.csv`](vote_share.csv) | 175 | Vote share of each candidate's party, by election and province |
| [`exit_poll_2022.csv`](exit_poll_2022.csv) | 24 | Exit-poll vote share of the two main parties, and turnout, by sex and age group |
| [`elected_officials_share.csv`](elected_officials_share.csv) | 18 | Share of the local offices won by each party, 2018 and 2022 |
| [`geo/provinces.geojson`](geo/provinces.geojson) | 17 features | Simplified province boundaries in WGS 84 |

The first four are hand-written lookups. The others are generated by the
scripts in [`data-raw/`](../data-raw); see
[How the files were made](#how-the-files-were-made).

## Data dictionary

### `provinces.csv`

| Column | Description |
|---|---|
| `province_code` | Two-digit administrative code of the province (`CTPRVN_CD` in the boundary file), e.g. `11` Seoul, `50` Jeju. The key that joins every table to the boundaries. |
| `name_en` | Official English name in 2022, e.g. `North Chungcheong Province` |
| `name_ko` | Official Korean name in 2022, exactly as in the original files |
| `label` | Short English label used in figures |

The short labels are the usual romanised short forms: Chungbuk and Chungnam
for North and South Chungcheong, Jeonbuk and Jeonnam for North and South
Jeolla, Gyeongbuk and Gyeongnam for North and South Gyeongsang.

### `parties.csv`

| Column | Description |
|---|---|
| `party_id` | Identifier used in the other tables |
| `name_en` | The party's usual English name; a literal rendering where it has none (`Korea`, `Unification Korea Party`) |
| `name_ko` | Korean name exactly as in the original files |
| `label` | Short label used in figures |
| `bloc` | `democratic` (Democratic Party of Korea), `conservative` (Liberty Korea Party in 2018, People Power Party in 2022), `minor` (every other party), `independent` or `other` (the combined "other" of `elected_officials_share.csv`) |
| `colour` | Display colour in figures |

`bloc` is a lineage grouping that lets the two main parties be compared across
elections. It is not an ideological classification of the minor parties.

### `elections.csv`

| Column | Description |
|---|---|
| `election` | `local_2018`, `presidential_2022` or `local_2022` |
| `date` | Polling day |
| `name_en`, `name_ko` | Name of the election |
| `race` | Which race `vote_share.csv` records: `governor` or `president` |
| `label` | Short label |

### `offices.csv`

| Column | Description |
|---|---|
| `office` | `municipal_head`, `provincial_council` or `municipal_council` |
| `name_en`, `name_ko` | Name of the office |
| `label` | Short label used in figures |

### `vote_share.csv`

| Column | Description |
|---|---|
| `election` | See `elections.csv` |
| `province_code` | See `provinces.csv` |
| `party_id` | See `parties.csv`; `independent` for a candidate without a party |
| `vote_share_pct` | The candidate's vote share, in percent, as recorded: two decimals for the local elections, one for the presidential election |

For the local elections the race is the one for the head of each
metropolitan-level government: the mayors of Seoul, the six metropolitan cities
and Sejong, and the governors of the nine provinces ("governor races" for
short). A party without a row did not have a candidate in that race, or the
candidate was not recorded (see [Caveats](#caveats)).

### `exit_poll_2022.csv`

| Column | Description |
|---|---|
| `sex` | `male` or `female` |
| `age_group` | `20s` to `70s`, labelled 20 to 70 in the original file |
| `party_id` | `dpk` or `ppp` |
| `vote_share_pct` | Share of the group's respondents who reported voting for the party, in percent |
| `turnout_pct` | Turnout of the group, in percent; repeated on both party rows of a group |

### `elected_officials_share.csv`

| Column | Description |
|---|---|
| `election` | `local_2018` or `local_2022` |
| `office` | See `offices.csv` |
| `party_id` | `dpk`, `lkp` (2018) or `ppp` (2022), or `other` |
| `share_pct` | Percentage of the officials elected to that kind of office who belong to the party |

### `geo/provinces.geojson`

One feature per province with the properties `province_code`, `name_en` and
`name_ko` (taken from `provinces.csv`), as RFC 7946 GeoJSON in WGS 84
longitude and latitude with six decimal places. See [Boundaries](#boundaries).

## Provenance

- **Election results** (`vote_share.csv`, `elected_officials_share.csv`) were
  compiled by the project author in 2022 from the National Election
  Commission's election statistics portal, [info.nec.go.kr](http://info.nec.go.kr):
  the governor races and elected officials of the 7th (13 June 2018) and 8th
  (1 June 2022) nationwide local elections, and the 20th presidential election
  (9 March 2022). The pages used and the date of retrieval were not recorded,
  and the values have not been re-checked against the portal for this version.
- **Exit poll** (`exit_poll_2022.csv`): party shares by sex and age group,
  compiled by the project author in 2022. The project attributes them to the
  joint election-day exit poll of the three terrestrial broadcasters (KBS, MBC
  and SBS), but the original file names no source and does not say which
  ballot the shares refer to or where the turnout column comes from. None of
  this could be re-verified for this version, so treat the table as
  indicative.
- **Boundaries**: see [Boundaries](#boundaries).

## How the files were made

The original files, wide CSVs with Korean headers and a shapefile, are kept
byte for byte in [`data-raw/`](../data-raw), next to the scripts that turn them
into the tables here; see [`data-raw/README.md`](../data-raw/README.md).

| Original file in `data-raw/` | Tidy table |
|---|---|
| `original/07_local_vote_share.csv` | `vote_share.csv`, election `local_2018` |
| `original/20th_presidential_vote_share.csv` | `vote_share.csv`, election `presidential_2022` |
| `original/08_local_vote_share.csv` | `vote_share.csv`, election `local_2022` |
| `original/08_local_male_age_vote_share.csv`, `original/08_local_female_age_vote_share.csv` | `exit_poll_2022.csv` |
| `original/07_local_other_vote_share.csv`, `original/08_local_other_vote_share.csv` | `elected_officials_share.csv` (named "vote share", but the values are shares of seats) |
| `shapefile/ctp_rvn.{shp,shx,dbf,prj}` | `geo/provinces.geojson` |

- [`data-raw/tidy_election_data.R`](../data-raw/tidy_election_data.R) builds
  the three tidy tables. It maps Korean names to ids with the lookups, drops
  blank cells, and fails on anything it cannot match. It changes no values.
- [`data-raw/build_province_geometry.R`](../data-raw/build_province_geometry.R)
  builds the GeoJSON (see [Boundaries](#boundaries)).
- [`tests/testthat/test-raw-equivalence.R`](../tests/testthat/test-raw-equivalence.R)
  re-reads the original CSVs with a separate base-R parser and checks that every
  value in the tidy tables equals the corresponding cell, with none missing or
  added.
- `make data` reruns both scripts. CI does this on every push, and fails if the
  result differs from the committed files.

The CSVs are UTF-8 copies of the CP949-encoded files first uploaded on
8 November 2022 (commit `056dfc9`, folder `datasets (preprocessed)`).
Converting those with `iconv -f CP949 -t UTF-8` and removing carriage returns
reproduces the copies exactly. The one other difference is that 13 empty
trailing rows in the 2022 governor file were dropped.

## Boundaries

**Source.** [`data-raw/shapefile/ctp_rvn.*`](../data-raw/shapefile), byte for
byte the file committed on 8 November 2022 (commit `4f79365`). It is 13 MB,
with 17 features and about 810,000 vertices. The attributes are `CTPRVN_CD`, `CTP_ENG_NM` and `CTP_KOR_NM`, encoded
in CP949. The projection, "PCS_ITRF2000_TM", is a transverse Mercator on
GRS 80 (central meridian 127.5°E, false easting 1,000,000 m, false northing
2,000,000 m, scale 0.9996), the same grid as EPSG:5179 (Korea 2000 / Unified CS).

**Origin.** Where the file came from was not recorded in 2022. Its name,
attributes and projection match the province layer that
[GIS Developer](http://www.gisdeveloper.co.kr/?p=2332) redistributes from the
Ministry of the Interior and Safety's road-name address map
([juso.go.kr](https://www.juso.go.kr)). The previous README credited the
National Spatial Data Infrastructure Portal instead. Neither attribution could
be confirmed for this version. The boundaries date from before the renamings
noted under [Caveats](#caveats). Their English names include a typo
("Jellanam-do"), so the GeoJSON takes its names from `provinces.csv`.

**Processing** (`data-raw/build_province_geometry.R`):

1. The attributes are decoded from CP949. Every code and Korean name is checked
   against `provinces.csv`, and features are matched by `CTPRVN_CD`, never by
   row order.
2. **Coverage cleaning.** Neighbouring provinces' borders in the source were
   digitised separately and criss-cross at centimetre scale. That leaves 8,353
   slivers claimed by two provinces (the largest is 0.24 km²) and 8,297
   uncovered slivers and pinholes smaller than 0.05 km² (the largest is
   0.048 km²). All boundaries are noded into one planar arrangement, and each
   sliver goes to the neighbouring province it shares the longest border with.
   The 27 uncovered areas of 0.05 km² or more are holes in the source itself
   and are kept. The largest, 315 km², is the basin behind the Saemangeum sea
   wall.
3. Islands smaller than 0.05 km² are dropped. Dokdo's two main islets are a
   little larger and are kept.
4. **Simplification with shared borders.** Rings are cut into arcs where three
   or more borders meet. Each of the 988 arcs is simplified once, with GEOS's
   topology-preserving simplifier on all arcs together and a 200 m tolerance.
   The rings are then rebuilt from the arcs, so neighbouring provinces keep
   identical borders, with no slivers, gaps or overlaps.
5. The arcs are transformed to WGS 84 and rounded to six decimal places, about
   0.1 m (five decimals created sub-metre pinch points). The result is checked
   for validity, both in longitude/latitude and in EPSG:5179, and for overlaps.

The result has about 21,000 vertices and takes 564 KiB. The total area is
100,065 km², against 100,058 km² in the source, and no province changes by more
than 0.4 %. The tests in
[`tests/testthat/test-geometry.R`](../tests/testthat/test-geometry.R) repeat
these checks, and check the join with the vote data.

## Caveats

1. **Unweighted provinces.** The tables are at province level, so statistics
   computed across them treat the 17 provinces equally, whatever their
   population. Correlations over 17 points have wide confidence intervals.
2. **Not every candidate is recorded**, so shares do not always add up to 100.
   [`tests/testthat/test-data.R`](../tests/testthat/test-data.R) pins each of
   these cases, so a change to the data cannot go unnoticed:
   - Presidential election: only the three main candidates are recorded
     (Democratic, People Power and Justice parties), to one decimal place.
     Their shares add up to between 98.1 and 99.1.
   - Seoul, 2022 governor race: the shares add up to 99.77.
   - South Chungcheong, 2018 governor race: the shares add up to 99.68.
   - South Chungcheong, 2022 governor race: the two shares (DPK 46.17, PPP
     53.87) add up to 100.04. Two correctly rounded shares of a two-candidate
     race cannot do that, so one of the values is probably mistyped. It is kept
     as recorded; check it against info.nec.go.kr before relying on it.
   - The 2018 municipal councillors add up to 99.96 in
     `elected_officials_share.csv`.
3. **Exit-poll turnout.** Turnout is the same for the 60s and 70s groups within
   each sex (73.9 % for men, 62.9 % for women). That suggests a single figure
   for everyone aged 60 and over was copied into both rows. It is kept as
   recorded, and figure 6 marks the repeated values.
4. **Exit-poll age groups** are labelled 20 to 70 in the original file. Exit
   polls often fold 18- and 19-year-olds into the youngest group, and
   everyone older into the oldest, but the file does not say.
5. **Party lineage.** The Liberty Korea Party merged into the United Future
   Party in February 2020, which renamed itself the People Power Party in
   September 2020. The analysis compares the LKP in 2018 with the PPP in 2022
   as the main conservative party. Minor parties differ between elections; for
   example, the Minjung Party of 2018 became the Progressive Party in 2020.
   They keep separate ids, as they appeared on each ballot.
6. **Races without a main-party candidate.** The LKP had no candidate for
   governor in Gwangju or South Jeolla in 2018, so there is no 2018
   conservative share for those two provinces; a swing there is missing, not a
   rise from zero. Jeju's 2018 race was won by an independent
   (51.72 %), with the LKP on 3.26 %, so Jeju's LKP-to-PPP change is not
   like-for-like.
7. **Elected officials** are shares of seats won, not of votes; "other" combines
   minor parties and independents, as in the source. For municipal heads the
   shares match whole numbers of the 226 posts exactly: in 2022, for example,
   64.16 % is 145 of 226. The council totals are not in the file, so council
   seat counts cannot be reconstructed.
8. **Names as of 2022.** Gangwon Province became Gangwon State in June 2023 and
   North Jeolla Province became Jeonbuk State in January 2024. Newer datasets
   give them the codes 51 and 52 instead of 42 and 45.
